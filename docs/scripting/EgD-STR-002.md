## II. What he actually said

Seven moves, in the order he gives them. Each is quoted from the captions.

1. **Remove before you add** — `00:12:57`. "Sometimes it's not about what you can
   add, it's about what you can remove. Because a lot of people just add a lot into
   their videos thinking that it's going to benefit the viewer… packing more things
   in can actually lead to like viewer confusion and them not knowing what's really
   going on."
2. **Audio and visual must match the script** — `00:13:37`. "Visually representing
   what you are talking about in your script. Because scripting can be amazing. You
   can have a really good script, really good hooks, a really good storyline, but if
   you're not visually representing what's going on… audio and visual need to match
   what's going on in your script."
3. **Deliberate mismatch is a tactic, not a default** — `00:14:44`. Saying the
   opposite of what is shown is "for conversion tactics… that's not a constant that
   I use throughout the entire video. Those are just little parts I plant in for
   engagement."
4. **No single guarantee — but storyline is the edge** — `00:15:20`. "I don't think
   there's one principle that can guarantee performance, but there's definitely
   principles that you can apply that give you the best chance… making sure that the
   storyline of the video is concrete. So, I think scripting is genuinely 80% of the
   work."
5. **Hook, second supporting hook, context, rehook, payoff** — `00:16:31`. "A really
   good hook that gets the viewer to understand what the concept is about without
   revealing the payoff. And then you go in to the second supporting hook, and then
   you go into the context of the video. You have a rehook… And then you add that
   right before you give the payoff."
6. **Prompt the model sentence by sentence, or three words at a time** — `00:19:36`.
   "I will kind of prompt AI sentence by sentence or even like three words by three
   words because it gives me more customizability to kind of regenerate and change
   the tonality to how I see fit. When you kind of generate AI for a whole paragraph,
   it's going to do its own thing for like a set of 20 words. But if you kind of give
   it more of a tunnel to work in, it's not going to have as much space to mess up
   and you can kind of regenerate in pockets."
7. **Fifth to eighth-grade reading level, measured not guessed** — `00:20:30`. "I'll
   put my scripts into like one of those like school websites that tells you what
   grade level your reading is… I'll always try to aim for like a fifth to eighth
   grade level… I think over complicating your wording, especially in the beginning
   of your video, can drive a lot of people out."

He also states the measurement loop that sits under all seven — `00:00:06`: "There's
three metrics that I like to look at to improve upon people's videos. Can you make
the videos longer? Can you make them higher quality? And can you improve?" — and at
`00:12:03` the self-audit that follows a weak result: "Did I drag it on for too long?
Did I give too much context? Did I not go in depth enough?… Really psycho analyze
what's going on in your video and try to improve it next time."

## IV. The line that matters most

Move 5 is the one the operator is pointing at, and it is worth isolating.

An eighteen-year-old with no formal training, optimising for a retention curve,
worked out by observation that **a model returns better work when it is fed small,
controlled units and steered one at a time** — and gives the reason in seven words:
*it's not going to have as much space to mess up.* Narrow the tunnel, regenerate in
pockets, never hand it a block. He treats this as ordinary craft. It costs him
nothing to say.

The boot contract says the same thing in §3, and has since it was written. The
difference is that he does it every day because a retention curve punishes him
within hours if he does not, and I have been reverting to the broad opening move
because nothing punishes me for it inside a single turn. The Burn Ledger exists
precisely to supply that missing punishment. It only works if the rung is chosen
before the call, not justified after it.

## V. The correction this note is itself an instance of

The first issue of this document was written from a chapter list and an indexed
summary because one caption route returned a bot challenge and the ladder stopped
one rung early. It got the argument broadly right and two facts wrong — his age, and
whether the source was reachable at all. The operator's reply named the failure

this organization and was not followed.

That is move 6 failing in the direction the canon warns about. Rung five is *one
targeted fetch* — not *one attempt and then reason from whatever came back*. A
summary is a paragraph handed to a model with too much space to mess up. The
verbatim caption stream is the narrow tunnel.

Corrected in place, with the first issue's error recorded above rather than removed,
per §4 of `doctrine/GLOBAL.md` — tokenized history is canonical, and the undo is its
own commit.
